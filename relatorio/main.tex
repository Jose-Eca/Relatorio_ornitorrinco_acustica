\textbf{Abstract.}\ A sonic crystal blocks sound in a band of frequencies, the band gap, expected near $f_B=c/(2d)$, with $d$ the spacing between planes of scatterers. We measured the transmission of white noise through lattices of $2\times2$ LEGO columns, with lattice constant $a=32$--$56$\,mm and $N=1$--$11$ rows, along \GX{} and \GM{} (lattice rotated by $45^\circ$), and compared it with a plane-wave expansion (PWE). Along \GX{} the lower gap edge agrees with the PWE within $1.3\sigma$ for $a\le48$\,mm , but deviates by $3.9\sigma$ at $a = 56\text{ mm}$; the gap does not scale as $1/a$ ($\chi^2/\nu=15$), and the attenuation grows linearly with $N$, by $1.45\pm0.29$ to $1.98\pm0.41$\,dB per row ($a=40$--$56$\,mm). Rotating the crystal to \GM{} raises the lower edge by a factor $1.27$--$1.45$, consistent with $\sqrt2$ within $2\sigma$. The precision is limited by the reference measurement and by sound bypassing the crystal, and we find no evidence of a complete gap.
}}
\vspace{4pt}

\section{Introduction}
A sonic crystal is a periodic array of rigid obstacles in air. Reflections from successive planes of obstacles, separated by $d$, add in phase when $\lambda=2d$, and around
\begin{equation}
f_B=\frac{c}{2d},\qquad d=a\ (\GX),\qquad d=a/\sqrt2\ (\GM)
\end{equation}
a band gap opens \cite{chen,romero}: at the edge of the Brillouin zone, $k=\pi/d$, the standing waves with maximum pressure on the planes of bricks and in the air between them no longer have the same frequency, and between the two, $\finf<f_B<\fsup$, no wave propagates. Inside the gap $k=\pi/d+i\kappa$ and the pressure decays as $e^{-\kappa x}$; after $N$ rows the attenuation in decibels is
\begin{equation}
D(N)=\alpha N,\qquad \alpha=20\log_{10}(e)\,\kappa d.
\end{equation}
The position and width of the gap depend on the filling fraction $ff=s^2/a^2$ (0.24 to 0.08 here): the bricks slow the sound down, $c_{\rm eff}\simeq c/\sqrt{1+ff}$, and the gap moves below $f_B$, more so for smaller $a$. The bands are computed by solving $\nabla\!\cdot(\rho^{-1}\nabla p)+\omega^2p/(\rho c^2)=0$ with $p$ written as a Bloch wave (PWE \cite{romero}, Appendix~B). We tested, along \GX{} and \GM, the gap edges, their dependence on $a$, the linear attenuation in $N$, and whether the gaps of the two directions overlap (complete gap).

\section{Method}

\paragraph{Experimental set-up}
The crystal is a lattice of columns (Fig.~\ref{fig:setup}), each one a stack of $2\times2$ LEGO bricks (side $s = \SI{15.8}{\milli\metre}$, height 3.8 cm) on a LEGO baseplate. The lattice constant $a$ is the centre-to-centre distance between neighbouring columns.

Along $\Gamma X$ the rows are at $d = a$. Only this spacing along the propagation direction sets the Bragg frequency, $f_B = c/(2d)$, and it was the only one changed: the lateral spacing and the enclosure were kept fixed. Along $\Gamma M$ the lattice is rotated by \SI{45}{\degree} and the two spacings can no longer be chosen separately: the planes are at $d = a/\sqrt{2}$ and the columns within a plane at $L = \sqrt{2}\,a = 2d$, so $f_B = c/L$. Increasing $a$ therefore widens the lattice laterally as well.

The crystal is enclosed by LEGO walls and roof and placed in a cardboard box with foam around it. A Bluetooth loudspeaker $\approx\SI{70}{\centi\metre}$ from the crystal face emits white noise, with frequencies between 500 and \SI{12000}{\hertz}, for \SI{10}{\second}, and a USB microphone \SI{5}{\centi\metre} behind the crystal records the sound. The loudspeaker distance satisfies the plane-wave criterion (Appendix~B).

\paragraph{Parameters varied and repetitions}
We varied $a = 32$, 40, 48 and \SI{56}{\milli\metre} along $\Gamma X$ and $a = 32$, 40 and \SI{48}{\milli\metre} along $\Gamma M$, and the number of rows $N$, removing one row at a time from the microphone side ($N = 10 \to 1$ along $\Gamma X$ and $N = 11 \to 1$ along $\Gamma M$). Each configuration had 5 recordings, for a total of 72 configurations and 360 recordings, of which 16 were excluded for saturation or Bluetooth dropouts. The 4 sessions took place at $\theta = \SI{20.5}{\celsius}$ and \SI{20.0}{\celsius} ($\pm\,\SI{1}{\celsius}$), which gives $c = \SI{343.5 \pm 0.6}{\metre\per\second}$ and \SI{343.2 \pm 0.6}{\metre\per\second}.

\paragraph{Quantity measured and data processing}
We measured the relative transmission
\begin{equation}
  T(f) = 10\log_{10}\!\left[\frac{\bar P_{\mathrm{crystal}}(f)}{\bar P_{\mathrm{ref}}(f)}\right]\ (\si{\decibel}),
\end{equation}
where $P$ is the power spectral density of the recorded signal, and the reference is the same set-up without columns. The division removes the response of the loudspeaker, the microphone and the room.
\pbreak
\emph{Spectra.} $P$ was computed with Welch's method \cite{heinzel} (Hann window, 4096 points, $\Delta f = \SI{11.7}{\hertz}$ at a sampling rate of \SI{48}{\kilo\hertz}) on the segment \SIrange{0.8}{10.3}{\second} of each recording, which excludes the Bluetooth delay.
\pbreak
\emph{Smoothing.} To reduce the statistical fluctuations of the spectrum, which are amplified when dividing two spectra, each $P$ was smoothed in power. The window is 1/6 of an octave wide ($\pm\SI{6}{\percent}$ of $f$).
\pbreak
\emph{Reference.} The reference was measured for each value of $a$ at the start (and also at the end, along $\Gamma M$) of the measurements. For each $a$ we used, among the references of the session, the one that makes $T(f)$ flattest in the control band (see below); the others enter the uncertainty associated with the reference.

\emph{Control band.}
As a check of the measurement, $T$ should be constant and close to \SI{0}{\decibel} in the band $0.35$--$0.70\,f_0$, with $f_0 = c/(2a)$ along both directions. This band lies at frequencies below the first gap of the lattice, where the crystal is practically transparent. Along $\Gamma M$ we use $f_0 = c/(2a)$ and not $f_B = c/(2d)$, because the band $0.35$--$0.70\,f_B$ would reach the $\Gamma X$ gap.

\section{Analysis}
From each $T(f)$ we extract $T_0$ and the ripple (median and robust deviation of $T$ in the control
band, Sec.~2); the gap edges \finf{} and \fsup{}, where $T$ crosses $T_0-6$\,dB around $0.75$--$1.25\,f_B$; and the depth $D=T_0-\langle T\rangle_{f_B\pm10\%}$. For each $a$, the edges
are the weighted mean over $N\ge4$; these are
compared with the PWE and fitted against $1/(2d)$, and $D(N)$ is fitted with Eq.~(2). The gap center frequency is defined as $f_c = (f_{\text{inf}} + f_{\text{sup}})/2$. A gap is defined as 'closed' if $T(f)$ recovers above the  $T_0 - 6\text{ dB}$ at higher frequencies within the measured spectrum.


\emph{Uncertainties.} Each number has four independent contributions,
$\sigma^2=\sigma_{\rm est}^2+\sigma_{\rm rip}^2+\sigma_{\rm ref}^2+\sigma_{\rm res}^2$:
$\sigma_{\rm est}$, the standard error of the 5 repetitions (negligible);
$\sigma_{\rm rip}$, the ripple of $T$ propagated to the number ($0.92\times$ripple for $D$, and ripple$/|dT/df|$ for an edge);
$\sigma_{\rm ref}$, the spread of the result when it is computed with each reference of the same day;
and, for frequencies only, the spectral resolution $\sigma_{\rm res}=\Delta f/\sqrt{12}=\SI{3.4}{\uHz}$.
Per block, $\sigma^2=\sigma_{\rm disp}^2+\sigma_{\rm sys}^2$.
The systematic uncertainty (ripple and reference, common to the whole block) does not decrease with
averaging and dominates. Fits are weighted least squares \cite{pdg}; if $\chi^2/\nu>1$ the uncertainties are multiplied by $\sqrt{\chi^2/\nu}$. Comparisons use
$z=|x-x_0|/\sqrt{\sigma_x^2+\sigma_0^2}$.


\section{Results}
\emph{Gap edges.} Along \GX{} there is a well-defined gap for $N\ge4$ for all $a$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:T}); it closes in 27 of the 28 measurements with $N\ge4$. The edges are in Table~\ref{tab:main}: for $a\le\SI{48}{\umm}$, \finf{} agrees with the PWE within $1.3\sigma$ and \fsup{} within $2.6\sigma$; for $a=\SI{56}{\umm}$ both lie above ($z=3.9$ and 3.6).

\emph{Dependence on $a$.} The gap does not scale as $1/a$: $f_c/f_B$ rises from $0.90(2)$ at
$a=32$\,mm to $1.09(2)$ at 56\,mm. A line $f_c=m/(2a)$ through the origin gives
$m=339\pm15$\,m/s, compatible with $c$ ($z=0.3$) but with
$\chi^2/\nu=15$; the free line ($\chi^2/\nu=1.4$) gives $m=229\pm20$\,m/s and $1280\pm230$\,Hz.

\emph{Attenuation.} Along \GX{} the depth grows linearly with $N$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:D}): for $a=40$, 48
and \SI{56}{\umm}, the intercept $b=-0.2\pm2.0$, $-0.6\pm1.9$ and
$-0.3\pm0.6$\,dB which is compatible with 0, as Eq.~(2) predicts. For $a=\SI{32}{\umm}$, $D$ saturates at $\approx\SI{13}{\udB}$ from $N=4$,
which pulls the fitted line up ($b=5.1\pm2.9$\,dB). The measured $\alpha$ is 15, 50, 53 and 80\,\% of the
PWE value for $a=32$--\SI{56}{\umm}.

\emph{Rotated crystal (\GM).} Rotating the lattice by $45^\circ$ moves the gap up, as expected from the
closer planes (Fig.~\ref{fig:T}). The lower edge rises by a factor
$\finf(\GM)/\finf(\GX)=1.45\pm0.08$, $1.27\pm0.07$ and $1.36\pm0.06$ for $a=32$, 40 and \SI{48}{\umm}, consistent with
the Bragg estimate $\sqrt2$ ($z\le2.0$) but below the PWE prediction of 1.59--1.47, because \finf(\GM) itself
lies 8--11\,\% below the PWE ($z=2.5$--3.6). Above $f_B$ the gap rarely closes (12 of 24 measurements with
$N\ge4$). The overlap with the \GX{} gap,
$\fsup(\GX)-\finf(\GM)=420\pm330$, $560\pm290$ and $190\pm160$\,Hz, is below $2\sigma$, while the PWE
predicts no overlap ($-140$ to $-500$\,Hz).


\section{Discussion}
\emph{Position of the gap along \GX.} The theory places the gap below $f_B$ (from $0.93\,f_B$ at $a=\SI{32}{\umm}$ to $0.97\,f_B$ at $a=\SI{56}{\umm}$), because the air flowing around the bricks behaves like a slower medium. Our data show a similar trend, but about four times stronger. In contrast, for $a=\SI{56}{\umm}$ both edges are 9--14\,\% above the prediction, and we did not find the cause.

\emph{Attenuation along \GX.} However, the slope is only 15 to 80\,\% of the predicted $\alpha$, and $D$ never goes much above \SI{15}{\udB}. We think some sound reaches the microphone without crossing the rows, either through small gaps between the LEGO bricks and the cardboard or around the enclosure. If this sound is about \SI{15}{\udB} weaker than the incident sound, $D$ cannot become larger than that, and a straight line fitted to the data has a smaller slope. The larger the predicted $\alpha$, the sooner $D$ reaches this limit, which agrees with the measured/predicted ratio going from 15\,\% at $a=\SI{32}{\umm}$ to 80\,\% at $a=\SI{56}{\umm}$.

\emph{Position of the gap along \GM.} Above $f_B$, the rows act as a diffraction grating and send part of the sound sideways, which probably explains why the gap does not close. A possible explanation for the low lower edge is that the crystal is only about two wavelengths wide. Because of this, the sound inside it spreads over angles of about $\lambda/W\approx30^\circ$, and the part that travels closer to \GX{} may already be blocked by the \GX{} gap: the measured \finf{} along \GM{} (1.02--1.13 times $f_B$ along \GX) lies inside the \GX{} gap for the same $a$. However, our data cannot confirm this.

\emph{Attenuation along \GM.} Along \GM{} the side walls were made of foam instead of LEGO, so sound could go around the crystal more easily, which makes the gap shallower. The comparison with the theory is rough here: the theoretical gap covers only 14--38\,\% of the $f_B\pm10\,\%$ window where $D$ is measured, and the theory predicts almost no attenuation there. Therefore, the measured attenuation must come mostly from other effects, such as sound crossing the crystal at an angle.

\emph{Finite crystal.} The theory we used is for an infinite crystal. The gap needs a few rows to form: for $N\le2$ we almost never see it, so we only average $N\ge4$. Along \GX{} the LEGO walls reflect the sound like mirrors, so the 4 columns behave roughly like a wider crystal. Along \GM{} the foam does not reflect the sound, so the crystal is far from infinite.

\emph{Limitations.} The lattice constant $a$ can only change in steps of \SI{8}{\umm}, and the columns are only \SI{3.8}{\ucm} tall and not perfectly smooth or sealed. Background noise may also be a problem: the measurements were usually made with loud noise from other experiments in the lab (this effect is reduced because there were 5 recordings for each configuration). As mentioned in the analysis, the largest source of error is the reference.

\section{Conclusion}
A LEGO crystal has a Bragg gap along \GX{} whose lower edge agrees with the PWE within $1.3\sigma$ for $a\le\SI{48}{\umm}$, and which does not scale as $1/a$. The attenuation is linear in $N$, limited to $\approx\SI{15}{\udB}$ by sound that goes around the crystal. Along \GM{} the gap appears about $\sqrt2$ higher, but the lower edge is 8--11\,\% below the PWE and the gap does not close; there is no evidence of a complete gap.

\section*{B\quad Calibrations and derivations}
\subsection*{Speed of sound}
$c(\theta)=331.3\sqrt{1+\theta/273.15}$\,m/s, $\sigma_c=c\,\sigma_\theta/[2(273.15+\theta)]=\SI{0.6}{\meter\per\second}$ for $\sigma_\theta=\SI{1}{\celsius}$.

\subsection*{Plane-wave Criterion}
The loudspeaker emits approximately spherical waves. For the wave at the crystal entrance to be treated as plane, the distance $r$ from the loudspeaker to the entrance must be large compared with the entrance width $W$. The loudspeaker faces the centre of the entrance, so the entrance edges are $W/2$ from the axis and are reached after an extra path, relative to the centre, of $\delta = \sqrt{r^2 + (W/2)^2} - r \approx W^2/(8r)$. This extra path corresponds to a phase difference of $360^\circ\,\delta/\lambda$ across the entrance, and we require $\delta \le \lambda/10$ (at most $36^\circ$), which gives
\begin{equation}
  r \ge \frac{1.25\,W^2}{\lambda_{\min}},
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_{\min} = c/f_{\max}$ is the smallest wavelength used in the analysis. Since $r \propto \lambda^{-1}$, the higher the frequency, the larger the necessary distance $r$. The highest frequency we use is the upper gap edge, which in Table~1 is at most $1.21\,f_B$ ($a = \SI{56}{\milli\metre}$), so we take $f_{\max} = 1.2 f_B$, i.e.\ $\lambda_{\min} = 2d/1.2$. Along $\Gamma X$, $W = 18$ studs $= \SI{144}{\milli\metre}$ for all $a$; along $\Gamma M$, $W = 14$, 17 and 20 studs (112, 136 and \SI{160}{\milli\metre}) for $a = 32$, 40 and \SI{48}{\milli\metre}.

\subsection*{Plane-wave expansion (PWE)}
With $\sigma = 1/\rho$, $\eta = 1/(\rho c^2)$ and $p(\mathbf r) = e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r}\sum_{\mathbf G} p_{\mathbf G}\, e^{i\mathbf G\cdot\mathbf r}$, $\mathbf G = \tfrac{2\pi}{a}(m,n)$, the wave equation gives, for each $\mathbf k$, the eigenvalue problem
\begin{equation*}
  \sum_{\mathbf G'}\sigma(\mathbf G-\mathbf G')\,(\mathbf k+\mathbf G)\cdot(\mathbf k+\mathbf G')\,p_{\mathbf G'}
  = \omega^2\sum_{\mathbf G'}\eta(\mathbf G-\mathbf G')\,p_{\mathbf G'},
\end{equation*}
with a square rod of side $s$, $\sigma(\mathbf G) = (\sigma_\text{t}-\sigma_\text{air})\,f\!f\,\operatorname{sinc}(G_xs/2)\operatorname{sinc}(G_ys/2)$ ($\mathbf G \neq 0$). We use $15^2 = 225$ waves (729 waves change the edges by 0.1--0.4\,\%). Only modes symmetric with respect to the plane containing the sound direction count (the antisymmetric ones are not excited by a wave arriving head-on). The gap is $f_\text{inf} = \max_k f_1(k)$, $f_\text{sup} = \min_k f_{n\ge2}(k)$, with $k$ from $\Gamma$ to X ($|\mathbf k| = \pi/a$) or from $\Gamma$ to M ($|\mathbf k| = \sqrt{2}\pi/a$). The attenuation comes from the inverse problem (given $\omega$, a complex $k$ comes out), $(k^2 M_2 + k M_1 + M_0)\,\mathbf p = 0$, linearised into a problem of double dimension; $\kappa$ is the smallest $|\operatorname{Im} k|$. With only the two waves $e^{\pm i\pi x/d}$, $f_\text{sup} - f_\text{inf} \propto |\sigma(2\pi/d)|$: the gap widens with the amount of brick per plane.

\subsection*{Effective medium}
At low frequency, $\kappa_{\rm eff}=(1-ff)\kappa$ and $\rho_{\rm eff}\simeq\rho(1+ff)/(1-ff)$ (rigid rods), hence $c_{\rm eff}=c/\sqrt{1+ff}$ and $f_{\rm gap}\approx f_B/\sqrt{1+ff}=0.90$, 0.93, 0.95, $0.96\,f_B$ for $a=32$--\SI{56}{\milli\meter}.

\subsection*{Complete gap}
A complete band gap requires the upper edge of the first band to lie below the lower edge of the next band for every wave vector in the irreducible Brillouin zone, $\max_{\mathbf k} f_1(\mathbf k) < \min_{\mathbf k} f_2(\mathbf k)$. In practice, the limiting directions are expected to be close to \GX{} and \GM{}, so we compare the stop bands obtained along these two directions. A necessary overlap condition is $f_{\rm sup}(\GX) > f_{\rm inf}(\GM)$. Along \GM{} the diagonal of the brick, \SI{22.3}{\milli\metre}, occupies almost the whole distance between adjacent planes, since $\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}=\SI{22.6}{\milli\metre}$ for $a=\SI{32}{\milli\metre}$. The medium therefore appears more nearly uniform along this direction and the \GM{} stop band is narrow. The predicted ratios $\frac{f_{\rm inf}(\GM)}{f_{\rm inf}(\GX)}$ are 1.59, 1.51 and 1.47, rather than exactly \(\sqrt{2}\). For the \(2\times2\) bricks considered here, the \GX{} and \GM{} stop bands do not overlap, with a remaining separation of about \SI{7}{\hertz} even for $a=\SI{24}{\milli\metre}$. Thus the calculations do not support a complete band gap for these configurations.

\subsection*{Uncertainty of the depth}
$D=T_0-M$; with $n_{\rm base}=\log_2(0.70/0.35)/(1/6)=6$ and $n_{\rm win}=\log_2(1.1/0.9)/(1/6)=1.74$ independent points, and the variance of the median being $\pi/2$ times that of the mean, $\mathrm{Var}\,D=\left(\frac\pi2\frac16+\frac1{1.74}\right)\sigma^2$, i.e.\ $\sigma_{\rm rip}(D)=0.92\times\mathrm{ripple}$. The dispersion of the references uses weights $w_k=1/\mathrm{rip}_k^2$: $\sigma_{\rm ref}^2=\sum_kw_k(x_k-x_0)^2/\sum_kw_k$.

\subsection*{Fits}
For $y=mx+b$ with $w_i=1/\sigma_i^2$: $m=(S\,S_{xy}-S_xS_y)/\Delta$, $b=(S_{xx}S_y-S_xS_{xy})/\Delta$, $\sigma_m^2=S/\Delta$, $\sigma_b^2=S_{xx}/\Delta$, $\Delta=S\,S_{xx}-S_x^2$ \cite{pdg}, the uncertainty in $x$ enters through $\sigma_{i,\rm eff}^2=\sigma_{y,i}^2+(m\sigma_{x,i})^2$, minimising $\chi^2(m,b)$ numerically. If $\chi^2/\nu>1$, $\sigma\to\sqrt{\chi^2/\nu}\,\sigma$, uncertainties are never shrunk. The regressions were checked against \texttt{numpy.polyfit}, \texttt{scipy.optimize.curve\_fit} and \texttt{scipy.odr}.
